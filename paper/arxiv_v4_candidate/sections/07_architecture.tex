\section{Transfer conditions and architectural implications}
\label{sec:transfer}
QB's indexing argument is independent of GPU brand and task; its implementation depends on tensor semantics, backend support and input state. The CUDA results establish a resident-request benefit. The Vulkan port and service panels delimit transfer rather than guarantee it.

For a new implementation, three checks determine what can be transferred. First, the query/mask map must preserve logical addresses, GQA and output order. Second, the backend must accept the resulting operators, and their actual placement must be observed. Third, stateful execution must meet the chosen reference at committed transaction boundaries. Equal outputs on a calibration panel are useful evidence, but do not make arbitrary cached-state components substitutable. A prospective extension should freeze these conditions before evaluating independent inputs and report accepted coverage, errors, rejected or unresolved cases, and full request cost.

The hardware question is whether greater execution freedom would lower cost. Independent queries can share data supply or physical resources while retaining a declared numerical evaluation. Reordering independent work is different from changing a reduction tree or its rounding points. An input/accumulator dtype pair is insufficient to identify all device arithmetic semantics; the PTX specification leaves some matrix-instruction details unspecified~\cite{ptx}. Our measurements do not identify an RTX reduction DAG.

Complementary designs operate at different levels. Batch-invariant kernels constrain numerical evaluation~\cite{thinkingmachines}; reproducible accumulation addresses order dependence~\cite{reproblas}; SPECTRA reorganizes tiled execution across speculative phases~\cite{spectra}. A new verification unit must improve on a legal software mapping and a same-resource baseline that permits reasonable K/V sharing. The scoped Vulkan repair resolves one support restriction in software, and PQ remains an essential comparator. The numerical reference must still pass after the supported dispatch changes.

A bounded companion AV study found no additional capability beyond its strong baseline. Its five mapping labels deliberately use one policy and yield parity; this is a consistency control, not a comparison of five independent architectures. It supplies no hardware speedup~\cite{studyrecords}. The subsequent replay, representation and service controls also establish no practical gain for the tested extensions. Shared data supply remains a hypothesis, not a result; candidate and baseline must receive the same reasonable sharing, buffering and scheduling capabilities.

A hardware claim requires a stable cost remaining after the software controls, a request dependency timeline, and a physical intervention identifying what can be reduced. For a serial, nonoverlapping fraction $f$ accelerated by $s$, the familiar bound is $1/((1-f)+f/s)$. No such $f$ is measured here. In particular, the earlier B570 observer's attention-core subtotal excludes projections and has measurable observer overhead; it cannot be substituted for a natural-request critical-path fraction. The current evidence supports a software-first design decision, while area, energy, frequency and system-level hardware benefit remain unmeasured.

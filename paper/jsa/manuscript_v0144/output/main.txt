   Reference-Preserving Speculative Decoding on Consumer Systems: Diagnosis, Repair
                                  Scope, and Paid Cost

Abstract
Speculative decoding changes both execution cost and the numerical history of a quantized runtime. When an application must
reproduce a fixed ordinary greedy output, the designer needs to determine where to constrain execution and whether the qualified
design pays. We study these decisions using natural donor-state crosses, a phase-scoped attention implementation, and paid ordinary
and matched target-only baselines. CUDA and Vulkan crosses distinguish local current-path, retained-state and interaction effects.
A separate B570 fork study finds unchanged immediate decisions but different trajectories under sustained alternate continuation.
On RTX, a query-layout transformation retains batched projection and feed-forward work; target prefill and continuation form an
inclusion-minimal passing set within a tested four-switch family. The frozen design qualifies full responses on 24 project-unused
inputs. Generation speed is 1.170 times ordinary and 1.169 times matched target-only execution. Resident-request speed is 1.742
times ordinary but 1.035 times matched: the draft-free path change already gives 1.683 times. Seven tasks regress against matched
request time, and one round reverses direction. Wider fixed-capacity drafting and a B570 endpoint repair qualify while losing
performance. Separate M4 and 358H cases limit added control and draft-only optimization. The study connects implementation-
specific diagnosis, finite qualification and paid comparisons to runtime choices, with repair evidence restricted to the tested Qwen3
family and configurations.
Keywords: speculative decoding, numerical consistency, execution state, runtime design, performance evaluation, consumer systems

## A–E delta and scientific assessment

- **A, historical comparator weakness: partially addressed.** Fresh direct-ID qualification is visually and verbally separate from historical text-path timing. The RTX positive qualification is now direct-ID evidence, but the old 0.650× timing remains historically scoped.
- **B, common replication: partially addressed.** The common workload/protocol family is clearer, while separate-study labels and appendix configuration differences prevent the figure from presenting a causal hardware ranking.
- **C, successful remedy: not generally resolved.** RTX serialization crosses qualification; B570 serialization does not. Neither panel demonstrates a new performance remedy.
- **D, Guard utility: negative rather than unresolved statistically.** Table 3 visibly separates the post-study paired contrast, whose interval lies above parity. The abstract and conclusion say Guard is slower than fixed speculation on that comparison.
- **E, scientific payoff: materially stronger, still bounded.** The qualification result now has a clean common endpoint across two prospectively frozen studies, making the empirical contribution more consequential than an anecdotal historical control. It still lacks a validated transferable prediction or a profitable bottleneck-directed intervention.

Scientific validity and contribution significance therefore diverge: the displayed claims are coherent and appropriately qualified; the remaining acceptance concern is the breadth and consequence of the measured finding. My minimum missing scientific contribution would be evidence that this qualification boundary supports a useful predictive or intervention result beyond the narrow tested configuration family. That is a future-study recommendation, not a request to reopen this frozen revision. The current title remains defensible, and no title change is needed for visual or scientific clarity.

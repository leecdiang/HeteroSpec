\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Output qualification, frozen prediction and realized cost answer different questions. Original direct-ID gates qualify RTX serialization and leave B570 blocked. On B570, frozen target-only UB1 predicts 72/72 capped runs across 24 repeat-stable prompts, including four reference-divergent prompts (12 runs) versus reference/live 60/72. A new same-state intervention changes scores and some later trajectories but no immediate sampled decisions. Separately frozen RTX H2 predicts 72/72 on another 24-prompt set while its UB1 reference matches 60/72; shared numerical routines and draft-dependent prediction bound that replication. New freshly qualified RTX serialization is slower (\FUCost$\times$) within its instrumented boundary. On 358H, combined replay/reconstruction plus proposal consumes isolated draft advantage without measured persistence payoff. M4 Guard preserves IDs and beats target-only (\RTGuard$\times$), but fixed speculation (\RTAlways$\times$) wins the post-study paired comparison; no incremental control benefit is shown. No unique numerical cause or portable performance law follows.

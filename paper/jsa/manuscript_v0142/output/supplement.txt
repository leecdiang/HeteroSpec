Abstract
This supplement records the output contracts, implementation boundaries,
campaign-level results, negative findings, historical measurements and missing
observations supporting manuscript v0.14.2. It preserves distinct task panels
and timer conventions. The accompanying author source package reproduces
figures and arithmetic from committed exports without model inference.
Verification is limited to inspected sources and exports; inherited original-
host raw audits are identified separately. Sections S1–S7 retain their topic
numbering independently of the main manuscript.

S2. Diagnosis: Predictions, Interventions, and Natural Crosses
S2.1. Prediction closes a different question from reference recovery
    The B570 target-partition predictor is frozen after 36/36 discovery com-
parisons. On 24 reserved HumanEval tasks, three repeats give 72/72 predic-
tor/native complete endpoint agreements, while reference/native agreement
is only 60/72. All four reference-divergent classifications and first-divergence
indices reproduce. They are HumanEval/134 at index 88 (914 to 1946), /42
at 73 (432 to 1817), /145 at 103 (1184 to 686), and /138 at 93 (14374 to 9064).
H1 passes its gate; H2 is not built in that B570 study. Stable predictive
disagreement is not repair.
    The RTX H1 transfer uses 12 discovery tasks with three repeats and
reaches 24/36 against a 36/36 gate. It stops before any reserved inference;
H2 is not implemented in that study. A later independently frozen proposal-
replay study evaluates 24 reserved tasks. H2/native and a fresh draft-free
R-only replay each reach 72/72; ordinary reference/native reaches 60/72. The
replay explains complete native output under its numerical routines, but does
not establish a cheaper qualified online correction. Its separately orchestrated
workers share pinned routines with the native implementation; they are not
an independent numerical implementation.
    RTX V4 has 19 HumanEval tasks, with P/R and R-only/native each 57/57.
Ordinary target-only UB2/native and UB1 reference/native each reach 51/57.
The same task panel is used for fresh timing, not a newly untouched cohort.
Section S6 records the host-policy amendment and preceding V3 termination
separately.

draft stay UB1. The protocol also samples and accepts the first pending
token and transfers KV, logits, logical positions and a post-accept sampler

72/72. Reference agreement improves from 60/72 to 72/72: four exposed

    RTX changes target continuation UB2 to UB1, leaving prefill, draft UB2,

S2.3. Same-state single-block intervention on B570
    The sampled-fork study uses the 24 reserved tasks, three repeats and
a frozen boundary at the first eligible verification block after at least 64
committed IDs. Two paths start from the same retained donor state and
process the registered block. Scores differ in all 72 runs, while immediate
top-1 and decision outcomes remain equal across all 195 internal decision
events. Downstream output changes in 18 runs from six tasks. The alternate
continuation retains later UB128 blocks, with proposals evolving under its
accepted history; the secondary result does not isolate one altered block
followed by restoration. Immediate agreement cannot stand in for complete
trajectory agreement. Runs and decision events are not independent additional
tasks; the raw verifier results cited here are inherited original-host results,
not a new private-payload audit.

S2.5. Bridge and offline selection limits
    The ordinary target-only U2 bridge compares 19 exposed tasks, three
recipes and three repetitions: 171 fresh arrays. U2 reproduces intervention N1
at 57/57 complete arrays and strict boundaries, while both match ordinary
reference on 17/19 tasks. U2 uses one occupied continuation token despite
UB2 capacity. This simpler reproducer limits claims that the complex replay
frontend is necessary; equal output does not imply equal numerical state.
    The offline selector feasibility study compares existing evidence acquisition
and decision rules. RTX direct enumeration and proxy acquisition each
require 114 equivalents and abstain under the measured criterion. B570
direct enumeration uses 72 while the proxy uses 144. These equivalents

S3. Implementation and Phase Qualification
S3.1. RTX query layout and dispatch
     The source-bound implementation modifies the non-flash attention graph,
not the quantized projection/FFN kernels or the greedy acceptance algo-
rithm. After the standard query permutation, a query tensor with dimensions
(d, q, h, 1) is viewed as (d, 1, h, q). KV tensors broadcast over the query batch
dimension. The corresponding mask is viewed with the matching layout; ex-
isting matrix multiplication, softmax, value multiplication and recombination
operations then evaluate each query column with the registered geometry.
Batched projection, FFN and output computation remain available. This
shape description is about graph operations, not independently traced CUDA
kernel dispatch.
     Assertions restrict the tested graph to Qwen3 non-flash attention, one KV
stream and supported query widths, without attention bias, sinks or MLA.
The initial patch supports up to four queries; the width study extends the
admitted graph to eight. Unsupported cases do not inherit qualification. The
earlier implementation creates per-query attention subgraphs before the later
query-batch transformation; their costs differ.
     The four-switch patch records model role and phase through a thread-local
guard entered before graph capture/fast-path selection. Target prefill and
continuation have bits 1 and 2; draft prefill and continuation have bits 4 and
8. The process mask selects enabled phases. A disabled phase returns to the
native graph. The graph parameters and reuse check include the query-batch
flag, preventing reuse across differing constraint modes. Reset re-enters prefill.
Thus the switches act on actual graph selection; they are not a retrospective
label on timings. Capture-off bypasses observer metadata and forced witness
synchronization. The optional continuation state-move helper is inactive in
the frozen expansion because its activating environment variable is absent.
     The exact scripts, native delta and reports are included under source_r
ecords/benchmarks/rtx_consistency_contract_v1 and rtx_consistenc
y_phase2. The author source package records their Git blob and SHA-256
identities. The portable manuscript code does not apply patches or execute
these runtimes.

S3.2. Factorial and scope-cost results
    All 16 phase masks are run on HumanEval/15, /18, /25 and /31 twice.
The task-level booleans in the original A1 result agree with the committed
16-row aggregate export. Passing masks are 3, 7, 11 and 15; all repeat stably.
Masks contain target prefill and continuation when they pass. HumanEval/15
and /31 pass throughout this family; /25 needs at least one target bit; /18
needs both. The main manuscript’s phase-qualification matrix shows every
mask and probe rather than only successful configurations.
    Mask 3 is inclusion-minimal among passing masks in this fixed four-switch
family: no strict subset passes. It is not a statement that every runtime must
constrain precisely these phases, that each bit is necessary on every input,
or that all possible constraints have been searched. Natural crosses identify
local dependencies; the factorial qualifies implementations under a different
complete-output gate.
    The independent scope-cost arm uses 19 exposed tasks, three conditions
and six balanced repeats: 342 formal requests and 18 excluded warmups,
capture off. Mask 3 speculation versus matched target-only gives genera-
tion/request speed 1.278076 × /1.112977×. Mask 15 versus mask 3 cost gives
1.003569 × /1.025686×, with 12/19 generation and 17/19 request tasks faster.
The extra generation interval is [0.997739, 1.009456]. Per-round generation
ratios are 1.008646, 0.998091, 1.004328, 1.003691, 1.007860 and 1.006777.
The predefined optimization gate additionally requires generation speed at
least 1.03, request speed at least 1.00 and at least four of six faster rounds. It
is not met because the aggregate generation gain is too small. Qualification
does not imply a speed gain from fewer constraints.

S4. RTX Expansion, Statistics, Width, and Reset
S4.1. Complete expansion and two timer boundaries
    The expansion contains exactly 384 complete unique formal keys: 24
tasks, four conditions and four repeats. All four conditions qualify 96/96 full
responses; ordinary reference is stable in all repeats. Lengths range from 11
to 128 IDs; 23 tasks stop at EOG and one at cap128. No extra full-stream
suffix is present in the selected response records. This does not certify any
unrecorded internal sampling stream.
    The retained server generation field is predicted_ms: the interval from
its prompt/first-sample boundary to the last generation update, with the
source’s minimum one-microsecond floor. It charges draft and target work
within the interval, excludes initial prefill and first sampling, and may exclude
final bookkeeping. Resident-request time is client wall time for complete
non-streaming response receipt and parsing. Model load, process startup,
excluded warmups, boundary telemetry and shutdown are outside it; post-
response asynchronous cleanup is not certified inside it. Neither timer is a
component profile. Subtraction of generation from request does not identify
a host, KV or attention cost.
    For each condition/task, take the median of four absolute times, form
baseline/candidate ratios, then their unweighted geometric mean across tasks.
Table S3 preserves all 12 timer comparisons. The ordinary-reference compar-
isons are post-hoc CPU analyses of already paid, qualified observations. They
are not new measurements or a new preregistered primary campaign. The
mathematical identity R/C = (R/M )(M/C) holds for 48 task-timer cells and
both aggregate timers; it is not an additive cost decomposition.

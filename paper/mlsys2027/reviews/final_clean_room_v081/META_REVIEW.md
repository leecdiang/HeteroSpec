Clean-room review based only on manuscript-v0.8.1.pdf and appendix-v0.8.1.pdf. No repository evidence, prior reviews, source files, handover documents, web search, or experiment records were consulted.

# Clean-Room Meta-Review

## 1. What both reviewers agree the paper contributes

Both reviewers identify the paper's scientific center as execution-path qualification under a matched generated-ID output contract, strengthened by a prospectively frozen B570 target-partition predictor. The predictor matches all 72 complete reserved serialized sequences, including 12 reference-divergent repetition pairs across four prompts (main pp. 3, 5, §§3.1, 5.2; appendix p. 6, §J). Fresh RTX/B570 direct-ID studies show that serialization changes qualification differently on the two tested paths, while 358H measures how a qualified but stateful interface consumes isolated draft-device advantage (main pp. 5–6, §§5.1–5.3; pp. 7–8, Figs. 3–4). Both distinguish these findings from historical timing campaigns whose M4/RTX/B570 equality checks use re-tokenized CLI text (main pp. 2, 4–5, §§2.1, 4, 5.1, Table 2). Neither reviewer treats M4 Guard as an improvement over fixed speculation.

## 2. Strongest contribution

**The frozen 72/72 B570 complete-sequence prediction.** It turns an observed qualification failure into a falsifiable, reserved-workload prediction of the entire output trajectory from target-only micro-batch-one execution. Predicting the residual divergent sequences and their first-divergence indices is more consequential than another speedup, slowdown, or equality count because it tests a specific sufficient execution partition before the live outcomes are known (main p. 5, §5.2; appendix p. 6, §J). This establishes configuration-scoped sufficiency, not a unique numerical root cause or a transferable rule.

## 3. Common blockers

### DECISION-CRITICAL

- **Comparator scope in the systems synthesis.** Both reviewers worry that a reader may infer a common strict-ID qualification status for the historical speedup rows. Table 2's caption and §§2.1, 4, and 5.1 expressly distinguish the text-derived historical equality checks from fresh direct-ID qualification; the issue is whether that distinction remains visible when the four-platform outcomes are summarized as one evaluation story (main pp. 2, 4–5, Table 2; appendix p. 6, §I). A precise first-page/Table 2 wording pass can resolve the presentation risk without changing the experiments.
- **Significance of a single-configuration predictor.** Both reviewers regard the four divergent reserved prompts and one B570 stack as the principal limit on the strongest result (main pp. 5, 10, §§5.2, 9; appendix p. 6, §J). The PDF does not claim RTX transfer or a universal mechanism, so replication is not missing evidence for the actual sufficiency claim. A selective reviewer can still judge the general systems importance differently; the manuscript should anchor its significance in the evaluation method and prospective trajectory prediction, while retaining the explicit scope.

### NON-BLOCKING

- **Narrative weight of M4 Guard.** Both see the three-part hierarchy on p. 1 and in Figure 2, but §7's lengthy policy account may distract from the B570 prediction and 358H cost evidence (main pp. 1, 6–9, §§1, 6–7, Fig. 2, Table 3). It is clearly labeled an exact, profitable runtime endpoint with no incremental benefit over fixed speculation.
- **Sequence-level wording on the first page.** Both see the frozen 72/72 predictor in the abstract. “Serialized output sequences” could more explicitly say *complete generated-token-ID sequences*, and the 12 reference-divergent pairs could be signaled earlier (main p. 1, abstract; p. 5, §5.2). This is a clarity improvement, not an evidence gap.

## 4. Disagreements

Reviewer A judges the first-page wording a minor issue and finds the warnings against UB1 universality and a slowdown-only reading clear. Reviewer B sees an important fast-scan risk: the title, four-device framing, Figure 1, and Table 2 can make the manuscript look like a benchmark, and “output sequences” may be read less precisely than complete generated IDs (main pp. 1, 4–5, title, abstract, Fig. 1, Table 2). The PDF better supports A's judgment about what a careful full-paper reader can understand: §1 explicitly enumerates the three contributions, §5.2 presents the complete-sequence endpoint, and §9 restricts the claim (main pp. 1, 5, 10). B's scan-risk judgment is still persuasive for a selective first read and motivates a small wording change.

Reviewer A emphasizes 358H as a complementary realized-cost boundary; B emphasizes that replay and proposal share one measured call and that the later persistence intervention did not reach formal performance evaluation (main pp. 6–8, §§5.3, 7.1, Fig. 4). Both readings are supported. The sound synthesis is that the tested stateful *interface* is expensive, without a replay-only causal estimate or measured benefit from removing replay. B also notes that the PDF reports, but does not itself expose a timestamped prediction ledger; this is a paper-only verification limit, not contrary evidence against the stated freeze (main p. 3, §3.1; appendix p. 6, §J).

## 5. Core-story test

**B. Execution-path qualification + predictive mechanism study.** The title leads with qualification, the abstract and §1 state the B570 frozen predictor, §§2 and 5 separate admissibility from cost, and §6 gives the consequent evaluation sequence (main pp. 1–2, 5–6). Consumer-system characterization supplies motivating cases; 358H supplies the realized-cost complement. The manuscript is not best understood as a generic device benchmark, a primarily heterogeneous runtime-cost paper, or an incoherent mixture. The extended M4 material is the main source of diffuseness, but its subordinate evidentiary role is stated.

## 6. 72/72 result test

**YES.** The abstract identifies the frozen reserved follow-up and all 72 predicted output sequences; the introduction calls them complete sequences; §3.1 states the pre-outcome freeze and success rule; §5.2 reports 72/72 matches, 24/24 classifications, and 12/12 first-divergence indices; the conclusion repeats the result (main pp. 1, 3, 5, 10). The appendix materially strengthens confidence with the deterministic reserve selection, endpoint, and prediction-before-live chronology (appendix p. 6, §J). It is visible enough to carry the novelty argument, although the abstract should say “complete generated-token-ID sequences” and disclose the reference-divergent cases to make its discriminating content unmistakable. The PDFs alone cannot independently verify the claimed temporal freeze.

## 7. Closest-to-rejection reasoning

The most defensible rejection case is that the paper's widely framed systems lesson rests on heterogeneous evidence grades: historical multi-device performance uses text-derived equality, fresh direct-ID qualification is separate and cannot certify those timings, and the most novel predictive mechanism is demonstrated on one B570 configuration with only four divergent reserved prompts (main pp. 2, 4–5, 10, §§2.1, 4, 5.2, 9; Table 2). Much of the remaining phenomenology—slowdown, batch-sensitive greedy output, heterogeneous placement, and cost accounting—is acknowledged as prior art in §8 (main pp. 9–10). A reviewer could therefore find the configuration-scoped prediction insufficient to justify the breadth of the paper, while accepting that the reported result is valid within its stated scope. This argument does not justify claiming leakage, a false 72/72 endpoint, or that the paper promises cross-backend transfer.

## 8. Closest-to-acceptance reasoning

The strongest acceptance case is that the paper explicitly distinguishes admissible execution from favorable component benchmarks, then prospectively tests an explanation at the level of complete held-out output sequences. The B570 result predicts both reference-equal and reference-divergent trajectories, while fresh direct-ID RTX/B570 studies show why serializing verification cannot be assumed to qualify every backend (main pp. 1, 3, 5, §§1, 3.1, 5.1–5.2). The 358H accounting demonstrates a distinct, qualified-path failure of isolated draft speed to become integrated benefit, with the expensive stateful call directly measured (main pp. 6, 8, §5.3, Fig. 4). The paper labels blocked timing as diagnostic, retains negative results, and states the limits of historical comparators and causality. This is a coherent systems evaluation method supported by one unusually falsifiable mechanism study, rather than a claim of a universal algorithm.

## 9. What should change before submission?

### MUST FIX

- In the abstract, call the 72/72 endpoint **complete generated-token-ID sequences** and mention that it includes reference-divergent trajectories; this makes the strongest novelty evidence unmistakable at first read (main p. 1, abstract; p. 5, §5.2).
- Keep the historical/fresh comparator distinction attached to the Table 2 headline and to any one-sentence synthesis of cross-platform performance. State explicitly that fresh RTX/B570 direct-ID qualification does not upgrade historical text-path timing to strict-ID-qualified performance (main pp. 2, 4–5, §§2.1, 4, 5.1, Table 2). This is a presentation safeguard, not a request to rerun the timing campaigns.

### SHOULD FIX

- Shorten or sharpen the transition into §7 so M4 Guard reads as supporting runtime evidence, with its slower-than-fixed result prominent, while the three stated contribution dimensions remain dominant (main pp. 1, 7–9, §§1, 7, Table 3).
- Use “sufficient predictor on the tested B570 stack” whenever “isolates” could be mistaken for unique root-cause identification (main pp. 1, 5, §§1, 5.2). Keep the appendix's audit and task-level details there; the main text already contains the acceptance-critical chronology.

### DO NOT CHANGE

- Do not claim UB1 guarantees correctness, identifies a unique kernel, or explains RTX; do not generalize the B570 prediction beyond its tested stack (main pp. 5, 10, §§5.2, 9).
- Do not fold new direct-ID qualification into historical speedup claims, promote B570 blocked timings to admissible performance, or claim M4 Guard improves on fixed speculation (main pp. 4–5, 9, Table 2, §5.1, Table 3).
- Do not require a new RTX replication, persistence speedup, or broad model-family sweep as a condition of the paper's actual configuration-scoped claims. Both reviewers treat broader transfer as a significance boundary, and no shared scientific blocker survives the existing evidence and a clearer presentation.

## Final decision gate

**READY AFTER MINOR TEXTUAL REVISION.** Both reviews find the main scientific claim intelligible and supported within its stated configuration, with decision-relevant comparator and first-page presentation risks that can be addressed in wording. No new experiment meets the required shared-blocker threshold. This classification is a manuscript-readiness judgment, not an acceptance estimate.

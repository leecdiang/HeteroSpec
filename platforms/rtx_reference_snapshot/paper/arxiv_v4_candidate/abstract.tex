Preserving a deployed quantized model's output constrains how speculative verification can be batched. We present phase-scoped query-to-batch (QB) attention, a graph transformation that separates queries through paired query/mask views while sharing K/V storage and retaining batched projections and feed-forward computation. Phase-aware dispatch and graph reuse bind the representation to the native speculative transaction. In a six-session CUDA study spanning two dates, QB lowers speculative request latency relative to explicit per-query attention (PQ) by 3.06\%, 6.17\% and 2.83\% for Qwen3, SmolLM2 and Llama, with complete reference-output agreement on the fixed panel. A 72-context replay establishes bitwise PQ/QB correspondence at QK, softmax and AV. A matched seven-arm comparison further shows that repair scope changes draft profitability: local repair makes drafting profitable for Qwen3 and Llama where global serialization does not, while SmolLM2 remains slower with drafting. Vulkan experiments identify the state and backend conditions for transferring the representation; a scoped grouped-query port preserves the existing AV evaluation while dispatching independent queries. Mapping and fresh-context service studies establish a separate deployment boundary: neither new four-input service panel establishes a practical QB-over-PQ increment, although their speculative policies have different net costs. Together, these results make repair scope, query representation and draft profitability distinct design decisions for reference-preserving inference.

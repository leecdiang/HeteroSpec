Reference-Preserving Speculative Decoding on Consumer
   Systems: Diagnosis, Repair Scope, and Paid Cost
                 Author information pending confirmation




Abstract
Speculative decoding can change a quantized runtime’s greedy output as
well as its cost. When a fixed ordinary output must be reproduced, a
runtime designer must decide where to constrain execution and whether the
qualified transformation pays. We study these decisions through retained-state
interventions, a phase-scoped attention implementation, and paid ordinary
and matched draft-free comparisons. Natural state–current-recipe crosses
reveal path effects and interactions on CUDA and Vulkan. A same-state
single-block intervention changes downstream trajectories despite unchanged
immediate decisions. On RTX, mapping attention queries into the batch
dimension preserves other batched computation; target prefill and continuation
are inclusion-minimal within a tested four-switch family. A frozen recipe
qualifies full responses on 24 project-unused inputs. Generation speed is 1.170
times ordinary and 1.169 times matched decoding. Resident-request speed
is 1.742 times ordinary but 1.035 times matched: the draft-free target-path
change already provides 1.683 times. Seven tasks regress against matched
request cost, and one round reverses direction. Wider fixed-capacity drafting
and a B570 endpoint repair qualify while losing performance. Historical M4
and 358H results limit the case for added control and draft-only optimization.
The contribution is a concrete runtime design study linking local diagnosis,
finite repair qualification, and paid cost, with failure boundaries retained.
Repair evidence remains specific to the tested Qwen3 implementation family
and configurations.
Keywords: speculative decoding, numerical consistency, execution state,
runtime design, performance evaluation, consumer systems

1. Introduction
    Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive inference by proposing
tokens with a draft and evaluating several positions with the target [1]. Its
appeal on consumer systems is practical: additional intra-request work can
amortize target execution when memory and traffic do not permit large
inter-request batches. Yet replacing scalar continuation with speculative
execution changes numerical call shapes, retained state, and bookkeeping. A
mathematically valid sampling rule does not by itself certify that a particular
quantized implementation reproduces a selected greedy output or lowers the
cost of doing so.
    We consider systems in which an ordinary greedy execution defines the
required reference. Examples include regression testing and repeatable appli-
cation behavior under a registered runtime configuration. The requirement is
token or endpoint equality to that execution, rather than a claim that its an-
swer is functionally correct or that every floating-point value is identical. This
makes two engineering decisions inseparable. The designer must determine
where execution consistency needs to be enforced, then measure whether the
qualified transformation is useful. Constraining the wrong boundary can re-
pair one input and introduce another deviation. Constraining all computation
can recover output while losing the intended acceleration.
    The boundary is not determined by the visible token prefix alone. Two
executions can have emitted the same tokens while retaining different nu-
merical key/value (KV) contents or pending state. Their next decision can
depend on that history, on the current execution recipe, or on an interaction
between them. Conversely, changing a score need not change the immediate
greedy decision. An unchanged decision at one verification block can still
precede different downstream output. These distinctions determine whether
an implementation needs to constrain state formation, current continuation,
or both.
    The cost comparison has a related ambiguity. A repair can improve the
target path before any drafting occurs. Comparing only repaired speculation
with the ordinary reference assigns that path benefit to the entire deployment
change. It does not isolate the value of speculation. A matched target-only
path, using the repaired target execution without a draft, is therefore a second
paid comparator. Neither comparator can be replaced by accepted-token
fraction, target-call count, or isolated draft throughput.
    Our cases show why both decisions matter. An RTX continuation inter-

vention repairs one task and regresses another while preserving aggregate
reference agreement. A later phase-scoped attention implementation quali-

   1. RQ1, diagnosis: does a deviation follow retained numerical state, the
      current recipe, or their interaction?
   2. RQ2, repair: within a defined implementation family, which phase
      constraints are sufficient to preserve the reference?
   3. RQ3, cost: when qualification holds, what benefit remains against
      ordinary decoding and against a matched target path?
     The contributions are concrete. First, controlled natural-state crosses
with own-path shams expose different local dependencies on CUDA and
Vulkan; a separate B570 single-block intervention shows why immediate agree-
ment cannot replace trajectory qualification. Second, an RTX query-layout
transformation preserves batched projection and feed-forward work, with a
four-switch factorial locating a sufficient target-phase scope in the tested fam-
ily. Third, ordinary/matched/candidate costs and qualified counterexamples
change the engineering decision about adding speculation, increasing draft
width, retaining a guard, or optimizing only the draft.
     Numerical path sensitivity, consistent KV state, fixed-shape verification,
and verification overhead have established research foundations. We do not
claim their discovery or a new general sampling algorithm. The additional
contribution is the implemented constraint, controlled evidence about its
scope, and the consequences for measured operating choices. The diagnosis is
an offline experimental procedure, not a validated automatic online selector.
The target models belong to one Qwen3 family; four hardware cases do not
substitute for cross-family repair evidence.
     Sections 2 and 3 define the runtime and contracts. Sections 4–6 answer
the three questions. Section 7 states the resulting design decisions and limits.
Supplementary material S1 gives campaign-level details, historical results,
failures, and missingness; S2 is the author source and arithmetic-reproduction
bundle.

2. Runtime Architecture and Research Context
2.1. Execution as a stateful transaction
    A speculative iteration advances a committed prefix through draft, target
verification, and reconciliation. The draft proposes up to k tokens. The
target evaluates the pending token and proposal positions, often through a
caller batch containing several tokens. Acceptance, correction, and a possible
bonus token determine what is committed. Rejected positions must then be
removed or reconciled before the next iteration. Stopping is evaluated under
the registered EOG and cap rule. Figure 1 locates the state interfaces and
the four phase controls examined here.
    The runtime state includes more than committed IDs. Target and draft
KV ownership, logical positions, pending logits or tokens, sampler history,
and loop counters connect successive calls. A pending token may already
have been sampled and accepted into the sampler while remaining to be
evaluated into the cache. A transfer that repeats or omits this event changes
the transaction. Early intervention frontends therefore retain pending state
and clone behavior as well as KV. The later B570 candidate instead binds
phases in the same context and live sampler. The RTX cost candidate does
not activate its optional state-transplant branch.
    The microbatch capacity, denoted UB, limits how a caller batch is par-
titioned for backend execution. It is distinct from k, occupied query count,
and the number of output rows reserved by the frontend. UB2 can process a
caller with one occupied continuation token. A larger k can lower caller count
while increasing the number of micrographs. Output reservation can change
the recipe even at the same UB. These distinctions matter when interpreting
a cross or a performance counter.
    The native implementation is based on pinned llama.cpp releases and
source-bound private frontends [2]. The late RTX graph uses Qwen3-8B
as target and Qwen3-0.6B as draft [3], both Q8_0, with non-flash CUDA
attention. The intervention uses existing ggml views and operations; it adds
neither a new CUDA arithmetic kernel nor a new acceptance rule. B570 uses
Vulkan with a different frontend and phase-binding operation. Metal and
OpenVINO-related historical cases retain their own runtime contracts. These
are software execution choices on consumer hardware, rather than controlled
comparisons of hardware alone.

2.2. Reference preservation and prior determinism work
    The output requirement is deliberately narrower than general determinism.
A token sequence can match while logits or cache values differ. Conversely, de-
terministic repetition of a candidate can stably reproduce the wrong reference.
We register the ordinary path and endpoint before asking whether a trans-
formation preserves them. Numerical consistency research already explains
why shape-dependent reductions can alter such behavior. Batch-invariant
inference constrains reduction strategies across batches [4].
    LLM-42 uses a decode–verify–rollback design, fixed verification shapes
with padding, and replacement of decoded KV entries by verifier entries [5]. It
also permits distinct consistent strategies for prefill and decode. Stage-Replay
studies fixed-prefix precision controls and bidirectional cache transplanta-
tion [6]. Vosti specifies and verifies deterministic inference at a system
level [7]. MarginGate uses margin-triggered verification and repairs confirmed
mismatches [8]. These works establish principles that this manuscript uses
rather than renames. Table 1 states the specific additional evidence and the
comparisons not performed.
    The natural crosses extend the experimental question to both naturally
formed state and the current native recipe, under a shared committed bound-
ary. They do not uniquely derive the attention implementation or identify a

                                          6
sole kernel cause. Local diagnosis motivates a candidate; native qualification
tests its sufficiency. We have no same-contract implementation comparison
with LLM-42, Stage-Replay, or Vosti, and make no superiority claim over
their guarantees.

2.3. Performance and heterogeneous execution
    Heterogeneous placement and draft-budget control are established design
directions. Dovetail and DuoDecoding exploit complementary CPU/GPU ca-
pabilities [11, 12]; TurboSpec and BanditSpec adapt speculation to execution
conditions [13, 14]. The published HeteroSpec studies contextual hetero-
geneity for verification allocation [15]. Our title distinguishes the present
reference-preservation question from that work.
    Lossless but Not Free examines consumer backend cost, cache choreography,
and cases in which verification fails to amortize target work [9]. Performance
or Illusion? studies speculative decoding in a production engine and shows why
apparent reductions in decoding work do not guarantee performance [10]. Our
measured extension is not that speculation sometimes loses. It is that output
qualification, finite repair scope, and the choice of ordinary versus matched
paid baseline lead to different engineering conclusions in an implemented
runtime. Historical M4 and 358H evidence constrains specific control and
offload choices; it is not presented as new causal replication of the RTX
layout.

3.2. Registering the output requirement
    Let R(x) be the output of the paid ordinary path under fixed weights,
tokenizer, formatting, sampler, runtime, backend, and endpoint. RTX ex-
pansion qualification checks the saved full response array, length, terminal
ID and stop reason, together with reference repeat stability. All 384 formal
rows belong to a complete 24 × 4 × 4 task/condition/repetition matrix. Each
condition has 96 qualified responses. References end at EOG for 23 tasks and
at the cap for one; lengths range from 11 to 128.
    B570 uses a different endpoint: retain IDs through the first runtime EOG or
through token 128, whichever occurs first. Its speculative response can contain
additional raw suffix IDs while satisfying this requirement. Those IDs and
their work remain paid. We report endpoint equality and raw-array equality
separately, including unknown historical raw comparisons. Historical re-
tokenized-text agreement is retained in S1 and is not retrospectively upgraded
by later direct-ID experiments.
    Qualification is necessary before a latency ratio is called a reference-
preserving speedup. A shorter, divergent response does not qualify, even if
its measured time is small. Repeat stability alone is insufficient: a stable
candidate can consistently disagree with R. None of these finite gates proves
functional correctness, equality of all logits, or preservation on arbitrary future
inputs.

3.3. Controlled state and current-recipe crosses
    At a naturally reached boundary, write the decision as
                                 d = F (p, s, e),                              (1)
where p is the committed prefix, s is retained numerical/runtime state, and
e is the current recipe. Cross two naturally formed donors s0 , s1 with two
recipes e0 , e1 while holding the registered prefix and current logical input
fixed. A within-row change identifies a current-recipe effect for that donor; a
within-column change identifies a donor-state effect for that recipe. An effect
present under only one donor is a local interaction. Discrete equality does
not imply equal continuous scores.
    The cross requires valid cache ownership and positions, faithful state
restoration, and compatible pending and sampler semantics. Own-path shams

4. Locating the Consistency Boundary (RQ1)
4.1. A repair and a regression under the same intervention
    An RTX intervention changes target continuation from UB2 to UB1 while
retaining the registered prefill, draft and caller grouping. On 19 exposed Hu-
manEval tasks, the source-bound predictor reproduces both native conditions
on all 57 repeated complete arrays. Nevertheless, HumanEval/15 repairs, /25
regresses, and /18 remains divergent. Reference agreement stays at 51/57 in
both conditions. An unchanged aggregate therefore hides opposing task-level
effects; it is not an all-task no-op.
    A separately selected 24-task MBPP panel has 72/72 prediction/native
agreement in each condition but only 66/72 reference agreement. One per-
sistent failure, MBPP/304, changes token 488 to 481 at zero-based position
33 while remaining a 47-ID divergent response. Prediction fidelity, response
change, and reference restoration are three different outcomes. The con-
tinuation intervention alone fails the intended recovery-without-regression
objective.

4.2. Natural donor state crossed with the current call
    Four exposed HumanEval probes are examined at frozen natural cuts.
Across repetitions, 24 donors support 48 state/current-path forks and 24
own-path shams. Donors retain actual pending state, cache positions and
sampler behavior. Own-path decisions reproduce the original execution,
and the original context remains unchanged after forking. Auxiliary short
continuations and their 1,278 forwards are diagnostic work, not a performance
panel.

4.3. One unchanged block does not certify the remaining trajectory
    A separate B570 experiment starts both branches from the same live fork
state, with byte-checked KV, and intervenes on one target verification block,
comparing UB1 with UB128. The frozen checkpoint is the first eligible block
reached after at least 64 committed IDs. Twenty-four reserved prompts with
three repetitions provide 72 eligible forks. All 72 paired raw score records
change, yet top-1 IDs and actual immediate decisions do not: zero of 72
executions and zero of 24 tasks show an immediate decision difference across
195 paired decision events.
    Continuing under the frozen policy gives a different result. Complete
downstream committed-ID trajectories change in 18 of 72 executions, cor-
responding to six of 24 tasks. Later alternate target blocks retain UB128
and proposals evolve, so the secondary result is not an isolated one-block
perturbation followed by restored execution. The score effect and delayed
trajectory effect coexist with an immediate discrete null. The intervention’s
primary sampled-block result therefore cannot be relabeled either an im-
mediate decision effect or a whole-output no-effect result. Its engineering
implication is direct: qualification must continue beyond a locally unchanged
acceptance decision. Neither logit movement nor one-step agreement replaces
complete registered-output checking.

4.5. Prediction and simple execution constrain added machinery
    Earlier prediction studies remain useful but answer a narrower question.
B570 prospective prediction matches 72/72 native arrays on a reserved 24-
task cohort even when reference agreement is 60/72. RTX’s first target-only
batched-path predictor reaches 24/36, below its frozen 36/36 gate, so that
study performs zero reserved inference. A later independent-proposal re-
play implementation obtains H2/native and reference-only/native prediction
agreement of 72/72, with reference agreement still 60/72. The first implemen-
tation’s missing interface is resolved by that later study; neither result makes
replay a cheap online repair.
    A fresh ordinary target-only bridge provides an especially strong simple
alternative. On the 19 RTX tasks, draft-free U2 reproduces all 57 intervention
N1 arrays and strict stop boundaries without a draft, transplant, proposal tape
or answer. It reproduces both /15’s repair and /25’s regression. This is output
equivalence, not demonstrated equivalence of unavailable live logits/KV or a
speed result.
    Finally, retrospective selector feasibility finds no additional supported
capability: RTX direct enumeration and proxy screening both abstain at 114
post-common-acquisition record equivalents; B570 needs 72 for direct verifica-
tion and 144 for proxy screening. Unknown counterfactuals remain unknown.
These costs are acquisition equivalents, not measured latency. Together, the

5. Phase-Scoped Implementation and Qualification (RQ2)
5.1. Constraining attention while retaining batched work
    The RTX candidate changes the non-flash Qwen3 attention subgraph
inside build_attn_mha. An initial implementation constructs one attention
subgraph per query and concatenates the results. A subsequent implementa-
tion represents query columns in the ggml batch dimension, allowing existing
operations to process them in parallel while retaining a one-column query
structure. Projection, output projection and feed-forward computation remain
batched. The change is local to the graph representation, rather than global
UB1 execution of every operation.
    After the existing query/key/value permutations, the query tensor has
dimensions (d, q, h, 1) in the supported single-KV-stream case. The candidate
uses ggml_view_4d to reinterpret it as (d, 1, h, q), preserving the source strides
and using the original query-column stride for the new batch axis. The
attention mask similarly changes from a query-column layout to (nKV , 1, 1, q).
Existing key-query multiplication, softmax, value multiplication and final
contiguous recombination remain in the non-flash graph. Shared K/V tensors
are not reconstructed from a reference answer. The purpose is to retain the
tested scalar-query computation structure while preserving parallel execution
elsewhere.
    The source explicitly restricts this branch to Qwen3, non-flash attention,
one KV stream, compatible mask dimensions, and absent attention bias,
sinks and MLA value projection. The initial query limit is four; a source-
bound later width build raises that assertion to eight without changing
backend arithmetic. These guards define the implemented family. They do
not establish invariance for every model graph, precision, shape or backend.
Native output qualification supplies the behavioral evidence.

5.2. Phase dispatch and graph reuse are part of the implementation
    The scope mask is process-fixed. A thread-local guard selects one of
four bits by model role and by whether the call is prefill or continuation:
target bits 1/2 and draft bits 4/8. It restores the prior active state on
exit. The guard executes before the capture-disabled fast path returns to


                                       15
native llama_decode, so disabling diagnostic metadata does not disable the
constraint.
    The selected boolean is copied into graph parameters and graph context.
Graph reuse is rejected when that boolean differs from the cached topology
key. This prevents a graph constructed for one phase layout from being reused
solely because the ordinary microbatch fields match. The server also resets
the phase to prefill when starting a new prompt. Scope selection is therefore
an actual dispatch and reuse condition, rather than a label attached after
execution.
    Capture-disabled costs bypass observer metadata construction and forced
observer synchronization. Qualification separately checks captured multi-
query nodes and capture-on/off behavior. The source also contains a con-
ditional target-state move after the first pending sample; the expansion
environment does not set its activating continuation-UB variable. The mea-

7. Design Implications and Limits
7.1. Constrain the transaction where evidence warrants it
    The useful outcome of diagnosis is a smaller design space. A current-
path effect motivates controlling verification geometry; a retained-state effect
requires preserving the state-producing path or changing its interface; an
interaction requires checking both. The RTX crosses justify testing a local
attention layout with target-phase switches. The B570 crosses motivate phase
binding within one context and expose why changing only current reservation
cannot repair a state-dependent case. Neither observation uniquely identifies a
kernel, and neither turns the diagnostic procedure into an automatic selector.
    These distinctions also determine what must be tested after a change. A
successful immediate argmax check leaves downstream state unconstrained.
A passing natural cross verifies one aligned cut, not the complete response.
The single-block B570 result demonstrates the gap directly. Complete output
qualification therefore follows local diagnosis and precedes any speed claim.
The endpoint contract must be chosen before evaluation: discarding post-
EOG IDs for B570 comparison is legitimate under its registered contract, but
all paid suffix work stays in cost.
    Locality is a property of this implementation rather than a synonym
for low overhead. The RTX transformation retains batched projections and
FFN while reshaping attention queries, and its phase switches restrict which
transactions use that graph. Mask 3 needs fewer constrained phases than
mask 15 to qualify the tested probes, yet the registered extra generation-
speed gate is not met. Fewer constrained stages may simplify a design or
reduce its exposure to numerical changes without creating a measurable speed
improvement.

7.2. Use the matched path to decide whether to add a draft
    An ordinary comparison answers whether replacing a deployed reference
path helps. A matched comparison answers whether the proposed speculative
transaction helps beyond its draft-free target path. Both are engineering
questions. Reporting only ordinary RTX request speed would hide that most
of the measured improvement comes from a qualified target-path change.
Reporting only the matched result would hide the measured deployment
opportunity. The ordinary reference remains a paid, qualified comparator; its
role does not imply optimal ordinary decoding.


                                      25
   The counterexamples change concrete decisions. On the frozen RTX
operating point, speculation adds generation benefit and a small aggregate
request benefit, with seven task regressions and a reversed round. The fixed-
UB widened draft should not replace that anchor for latency on the pooled
panel. The B570 repaired candidate should not replace either strong baseline
despite outperforming old N1. On M4, the measured guard should not be
added to the fixed policy on an assumed control benefit. On 358H, draft-only
work cannot remove the measured target-work deficit under the stated fixed-
progress budget. These are configuration-specific decisions, not a platform
ranking.

7.3. Scope of the evidence
     The repair study uses one target/draft model family, Qwen3, with finite
prompt panels and pinned runtime variants. It establishes neither cross-family
transfer nor lossless behavior on arbitrary inputs. The RTX project-unused
panel limits prior project exposure but is still a selected 24-input MBPP
panel. Wider geometry failures show that configuration changes require
renewed qualification. A second model-family study can be inserted after the
phase qualification and before the paid-cost comparison, with its own frozen
configuration and endpoints; this manuscript contains no expected results for
that extension.
     Greedy argmax is discrete and can expose small numerical differences near
a decision boundary. The observed score and KV differences do not separate
all reduction, precision, layout and scheduling effects. Full-response agreement
is an empirical gate, not a proof of numerical equivalence. Natural donor
states and own-path shams strengthen the local intervention interpretation
but cannot exhaust hidden backend state or establish a universal minimal
repair.
     Task medians and descriptive intervals summarize the saved campaigns.
They do not eliminate repeated-session, thermal, ordering or infrastructure
effects. B570’s missing old-N1 row and unknown raw comparisons remain ex-
plicit. Historical timing and energy domains differ; stronger later qualification
cannot retroactively certify earlier timings. The author-side CPU verification
checks the committed exports and source bindings, not every private raw
archive, an external peer review or a commercial similarity assessment. S1
and S2 identify the inspected material and the remaining author obligations.

8. Conclusion
    Reference-preserving speculative execution requires a qualified transac-
tion, not merely correct acceptance logic at one decision. The studied RTX
implementation constrains attention query layout during target prefill and
continuation while retaining other batched computation. Natural crosses
and a four-switch qualification study support that finite repair scope; com-
plete responses and width failures define its boundary. Paid ordinary and
matched comparisons then separate the deployment gain from the speculative
increment. The resulting decisions are mixed: the frozen RTX anchor helps
on aggregate, a wider qualified draft loses, the B570 repair remains slower
than strong baselines, and M4 and 358H restrict the case for added control
and draft-only optimization. The study supplies concrete implementation
evidence and reproducible cost comparisons within one model family, while
preserving failures that prevent broader claims.

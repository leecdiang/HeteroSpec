# Figure caption suggestions

Suggestions only. These do not overwrite manuscript captions or prose. Figure numbering follows the figure-redesign request.

## Figure 1 — Correctness qualification and realized accounting

An execution path first satisfies the frozen greedy token-equivalence contract. For an admissible path, speedup is accounted for by all committed output tokens per round, C, divided by realized draft cost, complete target-side cost and residual cost, each normalized by the matched baseline target-token cost T_b. C includes correction/bonus output; residual cost excludes work already included in the draft or target intervals. The target-forward proxy V_k^micro is measured separately and is not a substitute for complete R_target. This is an accounting identity at a consistent measurement boundary, not a new theoretical model.

## Figure 2 — Physical target-forward cost and path admissibility

Path-specific V_2^micro is the ratio of median width-two target-forward time to median one-token target-forward time. Points represent tested context/depth cells; connecting ranges span those cells and are not confidence intervals. M4 uses prefixes 512/2,048/8,192 with seven repetitions per cell; RTX 5070 Ti Laptop uses depths 0/256 and B570 uses contexts 512/2,048 with 20 repetitions per cell. Filled marks indicate tested correctness-qualified paths; open marks indicate blocked or diagnostic paths. Shape distinguishes batched/fast from serialized execution. The physically cheap RTX batched path fails the frozen workload gate, while its exact serialized secondary control costs more. Both B570 paths remain diagnostic; serialization improves exactness from 45/90 to 60/90 pairs but does not qualify the full workload. Contexts, stacks and boundaries are not fully matched across platforms. V_2^micro is a target-forward mechanism proxy, not complete realized R_target or end-to-end speedup.

## Figure 3 — M4 retains both negative and positive workload results

On the tested llama.cpp Metal path, representative Spec-Bench 24Q yields 0.842× [0.809, 0.877], with 72/72 exact pairs, while the separately preregistered HumanEval30 fixed-k2 study yields 1.387× [1.351, 1.422], with 90/90 exact pairs. Points are geometric means of within-prompt geometric means of three paired baseline/speculative decode-time ratios. Bars show 95% prompt-bootstrap intervals: 10,000 draws, seed 20260907 for 24Q; 100,000 draws, seed 20260910 for HumanEval. The 1× line denotes no speedup. These are separate workload studies, not a statistically paired between-workload comparison. The intervals are conditional on each frozen campaign and do not capture full session/thermal uncertainty. Exactness is generated-token equality, not functional HumanEval pass@k.

## Figure 4 — Isolated draft throughput differs from realized stateful cost

(A) Telemetry-off Phase-1 isolated steady-state draft throughput, reported as medians and 95% repetition-bootstrap intervals from five observations per device/context. NPU median throughput exceeds CPU more strongly at longer tested contexts. These steady-state measurements do not include the realized cost of reconstructing the committed prefix every speculative round. (B) Phase-3 arithmetic mean post-prefill decode time per request, across 90 observations per path on the frozen HumanEval30 workload. CPU and NPU totals are 13.25 and 27.54 seconds, versus a shared 9.58-second baseline mean. Replay plus proposal accounts for 12.63% and 58.35%; complete target-side work accounts for 87.34% and 41.63%. Residuals are retained without visually inflating their widths. Headline speedups, 0.734× [0.693, 0.771] and 0.352× [0.333, 0.369], use geometric means of within-prompt median paired ratios and therefore do not equal ratios of the displayed arithmetic means. Both paths are 90/90 exact. The OpenVINO call receives the entire committed prefix: replay and proposal cannot be separated in these measurements. The result is specific to this state interface; it does not establish intrinsic NPU inadequacy. Initial prompt prefill is excluded from Phase 3, so these totals are not whole-request user-perceived latency.

## Figure 5 — Categorical correctness and profitability regimes

The tested cases occupy multiple categorical regimes under the frozen token-equivalence contract. Blocked paths appear in a separate column without an admissible profitability coordinate; they are not negative or positive optimization results. Among qualified paths, M4 HumanEval30 is profitable, whereas M4 24Q, the two RTX serialized secondary controls and the 358H CPU/NPU paths are negative. The mechanism rows locate evidence-supported distinctions: workload conditioning, constrained verification paths, correctness failures, target-side cost and state-interface cost. B570 fast and serialized counts refer to 45/90 and 60/90 exact pairs; neither path is globally qualified. RTX denotes RTX 5070 Ti Laptop; its serialized CPU→RTX and RTX→RTX controls are each 90/90 exact. P/S/M/D denote primary, secondary, mechanism and diagnostic evidence; the 358H rows combine primary outcomes with measured mechanism annotations. Blank cells mean no case is shown, not an estimate of zero or proof that a regime cannot occur. Geometry is categorical, not a continuous correctness score or a hardware leaderboard. Cross-platform stacks and workloads are not controlled hardware-only comparisons.

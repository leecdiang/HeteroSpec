\section{RTX V4 Same-Set Prediction and Matched Cost}
\label{app:rtxv4}
The joint V4 study has two arms: A tests complete-trajectory prediction; B tests reference qualification and instrumented cost. They use the same 19 tasks, but every B observation is newly executed. A has 285 formal conditions (228 pre-live and 57 live); B has 171. The 456 conditions are not independent samples. Every named comparison has 57 pairs and 19 distinct prompts.

\subsection{Configuration, input contracts and ordering}
A uses Qwen3-8B/0.6B Q8\_0, pinned llama.cpp b10839, CUDA0 with target 37/37 and draft 29/29 layers offloaded, context4096, logical batch2048, F16 K/V, one sequence, 24 threads and FA off. Processes and contexts are fresh. Target sampling is greedy with seed123; draft proposals are limited to two and use the pinned native sampler behavior. The target-only competitor has UB2 and no draft. H2 uses separate draft P and target R processes; fresh R\_ONLY loads only target and sequentially reads H2's sealed tape. Native batched live uses its own drafting and accepts no tape. Numerical libraries and kernels remain shared.

R accepts a prompt at initialization, then only the allowlisted round, position, pending ID and proposals. It checks these against its own committed prefix and computes target sampling, acceptance, correction/bonus and rollback locally. Past-prefix assertions and tape integrity checks are not supplied future live answers. H2's proposals depend on its own earlier feedback, so tape construction is not draft-free. Application/API input separation and engineering rejection witnesses are retained; they are not adversarial OS isolation or an independent numerical implementation.

F0 fixes implementation, assets, effective settings, exposure, selection and gates before engineering; F1 fixes the predefined candidates before A pre-live. F2 seals all four sets of 57 arrays and full proposal tapes, with complete remote retrieval before native live. B has a separately sealed start state, timer and balanced schedule before all 171 fresh observations. Reported host/server records and Git membership support the preserved ordering, without independently trusted absolute process timestamps.

Completion uses a disclosed fan/host-policy amendment after predictor capture. The changed host policy is a study limitation, not an independently proved physically irrelevant adjustment. The original strict-host stop and predictor records remain unchanged. The author authorized continuation before any native live outcome; amended authority, complete predictions and tapes were sealed and retrieved before live. Numerical code, candidates, assets, gates, endpoints and cost schedule were not retuned. The same 228 predictor observations are reused, not a newly unexposed replication. The original stop and amendment remain in the full evidence packages.

The global native micro-batch option also propagates to draft; this is a comparison of whole execution configurations, not an isolated target intervention. Model, prompt, context, endpoint, logging, timer and schedule are matched. All 171 runs are fresh, although A has already exposed these tasks; B is a cost study, not another unseen prediction cohort.

Schedule seed2026100203 shuffles nine complete sets of the six condition permutations, plus three predetermined cyclic rotations. \[
\begin{aligned}
r_i&=m_{i,\mathrm{target}}/m_{i,\mathrm{serialized}},\\
G&=\exp\!\left(\frac1{19}\sum_i\log r_i\right).\end{aligned}
\]
The frozen 10,000 bootstrap draws resample the empirical distribution of paired prompt rows in the stated task order with Python Random seed2026100204; linear .025/.975 sorted percentiles give the CI. Floating-point differences are below $10^{-14}$; neither result replaces the preserved report. There are 0/19 ratios above one. Reciprocal GM latency is 1.476339$\times$; no CI is formed by dividing unrelated interval endpoints. The resampling is conditional on the selected exposure-audited cohort, observed condition medians and frozen configuration. It does not model exposure selection, the full HumanEval population or new sessions. The earlier 30-prompt 0.646 estimate remains a separate study, not evidence of an optimization trend.

QPC begins after synchronized initial target/draft prefill and before per-request sampler/state work, ending after final committed output, synchronization, response/log and slot release. Loading, initial prefill and teardown are excluded. A second chrono interval checks each QPC interval within $\max(0.020\mathrm{ms},0.001T)$; the APIs share the Windows oscillator. Empty clock-call probes do not bound all logging. Observer controls check IDs, not logging-free latency. Cost applies to the recorded instrumented request boundary; no throughput, energy or unrestricted deployment estimate follows. Both reference and speculative B conditions contain 48 earlier-EOG and nine capped runs.

\title{Reference-Preserving Speculative Decoding on Consumer Systems: Diagnosis, Repair Scope, and Paid Cost}
\author{Author information pending confirmation}
\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding changes both execution cost and the numerical history of a quantized runtime. When an application must reproduce a fixed ordinary greedy output, the designer must identify where execution needs constraints and whether the qualified design reduces cost. We study these decisions with natural donor-state crosses, a phase-scoped attention implementation and paid ordinary and matched target-only baselines. CUDA and Vulkan crosses distinguish local current-path, retained-state and interaction effects. A separate B570 fork study finds unchanged immediate decisions but different trajectories under sustained alternate continuation. On RTX, a query-layout transformation retains batched projection and feed-forward work; target prefill and continuation form an inclusion-minimal passing set within a tested four-switch family. The frozen design qualifies full responses on 24 project-unused inputs. Generation speed is 1.170 times ordinary and 1.169 times matched target-only execution. Resident-request speed is 1.742 times ordinary but 1.035 times matched: the draft-free path change already gives 1.683 times. Seven tasks regress against matched request time, and one round reverses direction. Wider fixed-capacity drafting and a B570 endpoint repair qualify while losing performance. Separate M4 and 358H cases test the cost of added control and the limits of draft-only optimization. These results guide constraint placement and the choice to use speculation after qualification, with repair evidence restricted to the tested Qwen3 family and configurations.
\end{abstract}

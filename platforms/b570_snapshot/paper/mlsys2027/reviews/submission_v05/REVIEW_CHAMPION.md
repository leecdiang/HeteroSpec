# Independent champion review — submission v05

Reviewed source: PR #27 HEAD `b326ff7f20d9bac5afff6f53a6e9d9d8a7aaa7ae`, now merged. I read `main.tex`, every section, the separate appendix, and the supporting value, table, and figure-caption sources. I did not read other reviewer reports. This is a narrative and contribution review, not an independent reanalysis of the measurement archive or a visual PDF review. No new experiment or change to closed evidence is proposed.

## The single contribution I could defend

**This is an empirical systems paper showing how correctness qualification and complete stateful execution change the apparent optimization opportunity for speculative decoding on specific consumer stacks.** Cheap batched verification and fast isolated drafting are real measurements, but they refer to paths or boundaries that may not survive an exact integrated execution. The contribution is the documented connection from that local opportunity to a qualified, measured runtime decision.

That is more defensible than either “a new controller” or “a comprehensive theory of when speculation works.” It also gives the negative results a shared scientific question rather than making them a collection of unlucky implementations. The most valuable cases do not merely report slowdown: RTX identifies a cheap execution path excluded by the contract; B570 rejects the apparently natural repair of serialization; 358H locates large costs in measured regions that isolated throughput omits. The prospective M4 study is a small test of operationalizing complete cost after qualification, not the principal performance breakthrough.

My champion argument would therefore be conditional: accept for a careful, concrete characterization of where component-level speculative-decoding evidence fails to support a usable optimization, if the manuscript makes those boundary failures its center. I would not defend acceptance on Guard's algorithm, its competitive performance, the breadth of four hardware names, or the bare statement that acceptance and throughput are insufficient. Those general ideas are already familiar. The paper must earn its place through the specificity and clarity of its measured counterexamples.

## The interesting insight

The useful insight is not just “account for overhead.” **The favorable component result can belong to a different feasible execution than the one whose integrated latency matters.** There are two distinct ways this happens:

1. Correctness qualification changes which execution paths remain eligible. On RTX, roughly one-step two-token target-forward work is available physically, but the measured greedy-output contract excludes the fast path. The exact serialized control costs roughly 2.14–2.43 baseline forward steps. These controls connect output semantics to the cost opportunity without identifying a universal CUDA problem.
2. The state interface changes the measured work. On 358H, isolated steady-state NPU draft throughput does not measure a synchronous proposal call that reconstructs the committed prefix. CPU and NPU then expose different dominant regions. This is stronger than saying “NPUs were slow,” and more precise than attributing the entire loss to replay.

B570 makes the first point scientifically less tidy and more valuable: serialization improves equality from 45/90 to 60/90 but still fails the intended workload. A small passing probe is not a certificate. The later 358H UB2 qualification failure reinforces this qualification problem under the tested stack; it supplies no runtime performance result.

The M4 contrast is a useful reason to inspect actual costs, but it is not a third explained mechanism. Different sessions and workloads prevent assigning its 0.842× versus 1.387× contrast to workload alone. A champion should concede that limitation once, clearly, rather than try to turn it into causal evidence.

## What Guard actually adds scientifically

Guard adds a **prospective feasibility observation about runtime information and execution accounting**. Its choice is frozen using separate calibration, it observes already-charged cost and committed progress, and it executes a real one-way transition with its cost inside the measured region. Its online inputs do not include future output length, the current request's target-only outcome, family labels, or the offline oracle. The held-out study therefore goes beyond retrospectively explaining where time went: one can use that information in an executable policy while retaining exact outputs on these observations and an aggregate gain over target-only.

The scope of that observation is small. Fixed speculation is already profitable on this held-out mixture and has the higher speedup estimate. From the shared point estimates, Guard's geometric-mean latency is approximately 2.7% higher than Always-Spec's; that arithmetic comparison does not provide a confidence interval for their paired difference. Falling back in 30.6% of requests demonstrates actual policy action, not beneficial action. The 1.0278× oracle regret is a descriptive distance to a hindsight comparator, not evidence that Guard improved on Always-Spec or approached an online optimum.

Accordingly, useful language is “a frozen realized-cost policy remained profitable on the held-out exact path.” Unsupported upgrades include “Guard prevents harmful speculation,” “Guard improves robustness,” “Guard selects the right regime,” “Guard bounds slowdown,” or “Guard outperforms fixed speculation.” None follows from the aggregate result alone. The calibration reference is a cross-prompt latency statistic, not the current request's true target-only rate; a cumulative threshold cannot by itself guarantee a per-request regret bound. Eight exploratory rounds can also incur costs before any decision. The abstract's phrase “bounded profitable control” is needlessly ambiguous: replace it with a scoped empirical statement, because “bounded” may suggest a formal guarantee.

The zero-margin winner and one-way fallback make the controller easy to inspect, which is an advantage for this role. They do not establish novelty. Nor does observed equality establish universal correctness of transition logic. Avoid inflating this small experiment to manufacture a systems-design contribution. It can close the characterization-to-decision narrative without becoming the paper's headline.

For 358H, the corresponding runtime result is a qualification decision only. Persistent CPU state was not qualified under the tested representation, the NPU state-capable route was unavailable with the frozen IR, and UB2 failed full qualification under the frozen target stack. No formal R2 performance run occurred. This is evidence that the proposed intervention was not eligible for the intended exactness claim, not evidence that persistent state cannot help or that the intervention would be slow.

## What currently distracts, and concrete cuts

The manuscript is much more careful than its central argument needs to be long. Repeated protection against overclaiming makes the contribution harder to locate. I would cut repeated qualifications, not remove their substance.

1. **Compress the evidence-class apparatus.** Explain once that primary, secondary, and diagnostic results permit different claims; keep status beside results. A full introductory taxonomy makes internal evidence administration sound like a contribution. Readers mainly need to know which paths pass and which timings are diagnostic.
2. **Merge the hardware synthesis into the runtime opening.** Section 6 repeats the introduction, outcomes, diagnoses, and conclusion. A short transition can state the two runtime decisions and explicitly leave the M4 contrast unexplained.
3. **Shorten provenance prose in the main paper.** Preserve a concise pointer and material limitations, especially the recovered runtime freeze-hash defect. Put bootstrap order-statistic conventions, historical empty scaffolds, reconstruction labels, and build-versus-formal-binary distinctions in the appendix where they can be audited. Do not conceal the defect; do not let it dominate the scientific argument.
4. **Move energy to the appendix.** The primary M4 energy interval includes parity; domains are incomparable; no runtime energy claim exists. This thread contributes little to the single contribution and costs narrative focus.
5. **Reduce numerical-debugging detail.** Keep the observed argmax divergence and its reproducibility in the main text. Token IDs 2790/2629 and exact signed score margins belong in a small appendix diagnostic. They do not identify the responsible kernel.
6. **Simplify accounting presentation.** Keep one complete cost/progress equation and a crisp distinction between forward proxy and complete target work. Move alternative aggregate reconstructions and exact normalized tuples to the appendix. The identity is useful bookkeeping, not a new model requiring prolonged exposition.
7. **Keep Guard methods sufficient, compact, and candid.** The main paper needs charged clock, committed-token definition, calibration/freeze separation, first decision after eight rounds, permanent fallback, and the three-condition held-out comparison. Tie-break ordering and hash-ranking mechanics can remain in the supplement.
8. **Trim figure-caption duplication.** Each caption needs its timing boundary, statistical interpretation, and decisive scope qualification. It need not repeat every paper-wide nonclaim. Any shortening must preserve the difference between proxy costs and complete round costs, and between arithmetic means and prompt geometric means.

This is editorial tightening using the existing closed record. I would not demand new experiments or new stratified analyses as a prerequisite for telling this narrower story. The resulting paper should openly remain a bounded characterization study with a prospective control demonstration.

## Independent proposed abstract

Fast draft inference and cheap batched verification can suggest that speculative decoding will accelerate a consumer system, yet both measurements can describe work unavailable in an exact integrated execution. We study this gap in four consumer hardware/runtime configurations under greedy token equivalence. On RTX 5070 Ti Laptop, correctness qualification excludes the cheap batched verification path; exact serialized controls lose its batching advantage. On Arc B570, serialization improves agreement but still fails workload-global equivalence. On Core Ultra X7 358H, an isolated NPU draft advantage disappears in the measured stateful interface: exact CPU and NPU paths achieve only 0.734× and 0.352× decode speedup, with complete target work and replay-plus-proposal dominating their respective costs. Separate M4 studies exhibit exact slowdowns and gains without isolating workload causality. These observations motivate qualification before realized-cost control. A subsequent 358H intervention fails qualification under the tested stack and receives no formal performance evaluation. On a qualified M4 path, a separately frozen one-way cost guard achieves 1.3114× held-out speedup over target-only on 60 prompts, versus 1.3469× for fixed speculation; both produce exact outputs in 180/180 comparisons. The guard demonstrates profitable prospective execution, not superiority to fixed speculation. Together, these case studies show how output qualification and complete stateful accounting constrain the translation of local speed into integrated post-prefill decode gains.

## Champion position

The defensible paper is about the failure of apparently favorable execution opportunities to survive the contract and the interface. Guard is a useful, modest closing experiment. If the paper instead asks reviewers to accept a new controller or a general account of speculation profitability, the available evidence will not carry that claim. The strongest revision should make the former contribution unmistakable while retaining the unfavorable Guard comparison and the qualification-blocked 358H result in plain sight.

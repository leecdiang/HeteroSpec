\subsection{Complete-output qualification across model families}
The larger confirmation tests the frozen target-phase repair on 24 inputs per family. Repaired target-only $M_3$ and speculative $C_3$ match all 240 required endpoints per family, whereas the unconstrained batched target-only path $M_0$ fails on 5, 14 and 9 distinct tasks for Qwen3, SmolLM2 and Llama. The ordinary $R_{\mathrm{prod}}$ recipe keeps UB1 for Qwen3 and uses UB512 for the other families. \subsection{Same-input correspondence across layers and phases}
\label{sec:numeric}
The initial 24-context replay established PQ/QB correspondence at sampled layer-0 attention boundaries. E1 expands the test to three layer positions, prefill and continuation, and $q=1,2,3,4$ in each family. All 72 contexts pass observation, native-MQ own-path and repeatability checks. PQ and QB are bitwise equal at QK, softmax and AV in every context, with zero maximum error and ULP distance (Figure~\ref{fig:numeric}).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering

\caption{Completed 72-context CUDA replay. The 18 agreements between MQ and either repair are exactly the $q=1$ common-path controls. The 54 multi-query cases first differ at QK. All PQ/QB stage outputs agree bitwise.}
\label{fig:numeric}
\end{figure}

The sampled target layers are $0/18/35$ for Qwen3, $0/12/23$ for SmolLM2 and $0/16/31$ for Llama. Qwen3 and Llama use head dimension 128 with 32 query heads and 8 KV heads; SmolLM2 uses dimension 64 with 32/32 heads. Thus the observed correspondence spans both ordinary and grouped-query attention and more than one head dimension.

MQ agrees with either repaired path only in the 18 single-query controls. Across the other 54 contexts, its first difference is at QK; maximum finite absolute differences are 0.078583 at QK, 0.000917 at softmax and 0.010433 at AV. These are operator differences on identical operands, not model-logit bounds.

All snapshots have tensor key extent 256, including masked padding. The maximum number of visible keys is 109, 130 and 127 by family; prefill captures include early query positions with only one to four visible keys. The experiment covers layers, phases and query widths at these short visible contexts, using one exposed prompt per family. Complete coordinates and operator comparisons appear in \sref{supp:cuda}.

\subsection{QB's independent implementation benefit}
\label{sec:e2}
E2 completes 1,620 requests in 324 fresh workers, including 324 registered warmups. There are no request failures, output mismatches, workload errors or heavy-observer flags. The six-session speculative ratios $C_{\PQ}/C_{\QB}$ are 1.031614, 1.065803 and 1.029114, corresponding to 3.06\%, 6.17\% and 2.83\% lower QB request latency. Figure~\ref{fig:e2} shows both paths and their session-level uncertainty.

\begin{figure*}[t]\centering
\input{figures/sessions}
\caption{Independent-block E2 replication. Small marks are the six session estimates; large markers and horizontal bars are the equal-weight estimate and its registered 95\% six-session percentile interval. Dashed lines mark parity and the 1.02 practical threshold. $M$ is target-only and $C$ speculative. The fixed exposed panel and single host limit statistical scope.}
\label{fig:e2}
\end{figure*}

All 36 family/path/session contrasts favor QB. This agreement coexists with request-level variation: 9 of the 72 family/path/task aggregates regress. The speculative intervals are [1.025393,1.037926], [1.063387,1.068399] and [1.020126,1.039149], each above the predeclared 1.02 threshold. Target-only effects are smaller: Llama's estimate is 1.015593, below the threshold, while Qwen3's interval overlaps it. The complete estimates, task regressions and session times appear in \sref{supp:cuda}.

Recorded acceptance, rollback and logical-workload counters agree in all 216 speculative PQ/QB task/session pairs. Together with endpoint agreement, this supports an implementation-cost comparison rather than a gain caused by different recorded speculative work. The recorded counters cover acceptance, rollback and logical work rather than full control traces. The earlier two-adjacent-session results remain in \sref{supp:cost}, including the target-only Qwen3 and Llama direction reversals.

\subsection{Repair scope changes draft profitability}
\label{sec:economics}
The seven-arm campaign compares repair scope and drafting on the same qualified 12-input panel. Figure~\ref{fig:cost} separates local representation cost, global serialization cost and the draft increment under all three repair organizations. These estimates retain the original two-session protocol, separately from E2.

\begin{figure*}[t]\centering
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/cost}}
\caption{Matched repair and drafting choices in the original seven-arm campaign, separate from E2. A/B: speculative PQ and GS costs relative to QB. C: draft value under each repair organization, $D_L=T_{M_L}/T_{C_L}$; values above one favor drafting. Qwen3 and Llama cross parity under both local repairs, while SmolLM2 remains below it. GS/PQ values in C are reconstructed by Equation~\ref{eq:draft} from the common-panel ratios. All points are descriptive estimates; exact source ratios and every regression are retained in \sref{supp:cost}.}
\label{fig:cost}
\end{figure*}

Its speculative GS/QB ratios are 2.144, 1.463 and 2.176; every task favors QB. These differences reflect the value of constraining attention locally while retaining batched projection and feed-forward work. The smaller PQ/QB ratios, 1.044, 1.068 and 1.022, isolate the representation choice within local repair. They are not multiplied into E2 or treated as successive software-version improvements.

Adding the draft to QB is profitable for Qwen3 and Llama but not SmolLM2: $M_{\QB}/C_{\QB}$ is 1.144, 0.634 and 1.117. SmolLM2 takes about 1.58 times its own draft-free request duration despite QB being cheaper than PQ. Across the common seven-arm panel, draft value under another layout $L$ follows
\begin{equation}
D_L=\frac{S_{M_L/M_{\QB}}}{S_{C_L/C_{\QB}}}\,D_{\QB},\qquad D_{\QB}=S_{M_{\QB}/C_{\QB}}.
\label{eq:draft}
\end{equation}
The derived GS/PQ/QB values are 0.710/1.125/1.144 for Qwen3, 0.592/0.616/0.634 for SmolLM2 and 0.724/1.107/1.117 for Llama. Thus the repair policy changes the sign of the draft decision for two families. PQ already makes that transition; QB adds the smaller representation benefit.

The relative change in draft value summarizes this interaction:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\QB\leftarrow\GS}=\frac{D_{\QB}}{D_{\GS}}
=\frac{S_{C_{\GS}/C_{\QB}}}{S_{M_{\GS}/M_{\QB}}}.
\label{eq:repairinteraction}
\end{equation}
It is 1.612, 1.071 and 1.542 for the three families, respectively. These are retrospective ratio-of-ratios summaries from the reported common-panel estimates, with the original weights and timer. They measure the observed repair--draft interaction, not isolated operator costs. The corresponding design decision is to evaluate the draft after selecting a qualified repair policy, rather than treating draft profitability as a model-pair property alone.

Target-only QB also preserves the specified serial reference at lower cost: $R/M_{\QB}$ is 1.334051, 1.369890 and 1.426161, with every task favoring QB on this panel. SmolLM2 therefore benefits from reference-preserving batching even when drafting is unprofitable.

A historical fixed-UB4 experiment provides a related counterexample: increasing $k$ from 3 to 7 preserved outputs but reduced request speed to 0.890 of the smaller-draft path. Separate work captures recorded fewer target callers and more physical micrographs. Acceptance and caller reduction alone therefore cannot establish request acceleration. The width failures and negative cost results that constrain this conclusion remain in \sref{supp:implementation}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Attention query geometry need not be tied to target-wide batching. The phase-scoped query layout evaluated here separates those choices through an explicit query/mask view, native K/V and GQA semantics, ordered recombination, and layout-sensitive phase/cache control. Fixed-input CUDA replay establishes bitwise PQ/QB correspondence at sampled attention stages, while separate three-family experiments establish complete registered-output qualification. Under that same reference requirement, QB reduces measured aggregate request cost relative to per-query attention and global target serialization. Adding a draft remains a separate decision and is unprofitable for one of the three tested families.

The complementary Vulkan interventions show why the method requires an explicit numerical scope: prefill partitioning can change projections and retained state, and qualified KV-source interventions can change later output even under one-query continuation. This supports phase-aware validation of the whole stateful execution, not a universal attention-only repair rule. The resulting contribution is a specified and empirically qualified runtime method with measured implementation advantages and concrete failure boundaries, rather than an unrestricted determinism guarantee or an unconditional acceleration claim.

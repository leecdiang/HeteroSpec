\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Attention query geometry can be controlled independently of target-wide batching. Our phase-scoped layout combines query/mask views, native K/V and GQA semantics, ordered recombination, and phase-aware graph reuse. Fixed-input CUDA replay establishes sampled bitwise PQ/QB correspondence; separate three-family experiments establish complete registered-output qualification. QB lowers aggregate request cost relative to PQ and global serialization under that common reference. Target-only PQ/QB costs remain session-sensitive for Qwen3 and Llama, and adding a draft is unprofitable for SmolLM2.

Vulkan interventions delimit the design's scope. Actual prefill partitions can change projections and retained state, and a qualified KV-source intervention changes later output despite one-query continuation. For the evaluated stacks, these findings connect the scope of a local constraint to the numerical frontier and the state carried into continuation. Phase-scoped query-to-batch execution provides a concrete way to retain surrounding batching when attention control passes complete-output qualification; its repair cost and the value of adding a draft remain separate measured decisions.

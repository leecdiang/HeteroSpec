          Reference-Compatible Parallel Execution
               for Quantized LLM Inference
                                                        Chengzhan Li
                                    University of Electronic Science and Technology of China
                                                         Chengdu, China
                                                  chengzhan.li@std.uestc.edu.cn


     Abstract—Batching a quantized language model can change its numerical output even when token positions and causal
     masks stay the same. We introduce reference-derived phase plans for parallel inference that retains an established
     deployment’s numerical behavior. A plan binds the prompt producer, physical KV range, arithmetic recipe and ownership
     of outputs and state. It places cuts around whole graphs or within attention, while packing independent arithmetic groups
     into wider operations. Implementations on CUDA and Vulkan cover Qwen, Llama and SmolLM2, including a mixed-Q4
     32B model. On Linux/A5000, compatible speculative applications reduce resident-service time by 17.21%, 25.54% and
     11.52% against the fastest measured fixed target-only control for Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B and Llama-8B. Separate balanced
     experiments attribute 0.80%–1.64% to Q8 projection packing; local cuts contribute smaller, geometry-dependent gains. For
     32B mixed quantization, independent-channel grouping reduces ordinary service time by 5.61% relative to a graph-level
     scalar-column loop. Eight-query execution also lowers aggregate cost at equal work, while longer live proposals add
     enough work to favor the shorter configuration. Phase plans thus preserve reference-sensitive computation while exposing
     parallel work, with complete-request costs guiding the choice of execution strategy.

     Index Terms—Parallel inference, quantized language models, numerical compatibility, execution planning, speculative
     decoding.

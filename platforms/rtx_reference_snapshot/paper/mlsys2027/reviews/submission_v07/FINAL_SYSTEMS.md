## Overall assessment

The paper now has a substantially stronger empirical qualification result. Two prospectively frozen studies show different responses to serialization under an explicit output contract. This improves the systems contribution beyond a collection of historical text-comparison outcomes. The direct-ID endpoints are credible within their documented configurations, and the paper keeps these new results separate from old timing. My remaining objection concerns scientific payoff at a selective general MLSys bar, rather than a demonstrated invalid result.

## Strengths and exact scope

The strongest addition is a concrete operational boundary. RTX batching matches 75/90 sequences, whereas serialization reaches 90/90 and qualifies. B570 improves from 60/90 to 78/90 and remains blocked. Both complete 270 executions and 180 comparisons, retain mismatches, and show repeat-stable outputs. Three matching repetitions are not three independent demonstrations of population generality; nevertheless, their consistency helps distinguish reproducible path disagreement from erratic capture. The validation receipts document independent extraction/comparison and archive checks, with appropriately explicit limits on omitted external assets.

The retained 358H measurements are also useful. Complete target-side work consumes 87.34% of CPU-draft speculative time; the NPU path spends 58.35% in the measured replay-plus-proposal call. Small residual costs prevent orchestration from being casually blamed for the major loss. Separating forward-only proxies, accepted proposals, committed output and complete timed regions makes the negative results interpretable. The paper correctly refuses to manufacture a replay-only component or persistence benefit.

The M4 study supplies direct-ID-preserving prospective execution on held-out prompts, and it includes fixed speculation. The audited metadata exception, retained stop markers, differing estimators, timing exclusions and secondary-analysis status are visible. These qualifications improve trust without turning every historical provenance boundary into an integrity allegation.

## Five issues determining the acceptance case

**A. Historical comparator weakness.** The new studies partially address this prospectively. They establish a direct-ID-qualified RTX alternative in the new configuration; they do not repair historical RTX timing or historical M4 equality. The fresh RTX driver differs, and B570's new counts are not replacements for historical 45/90 and 60/90 text-path counts. The uniform strict-contract performance story therefore remains unavailable. Figure 3's separation of historical cost and prospective qualification is essential, not merely cautious wording.

**B. Common replication.** There is meaningful replication of the qualification question: the same HumanEval30 IDs, Qwen3-8B/0.6B Q8_0 models, greedy k=2, three repetitions and 90/90 gate. It is stronger than four unrelated cases. However, RTX uses the embedded-server CLI, context 4096 and reference micro-batch one; B570 uses instrumented completion/speculative front-ends, context 1024 and reference micro-batch 128. Their different capture boundaries and frozen references prevent causal architecture ranking or attribution of the different responses to CUDA versus Vulkan alone. They replicate an operational question, not a common causal experiment.

**C. Successful remedy.** RTX serialization is a successful qualification remedy on its tested path. It is not a demonstrated performance remedy: no new formal performance endpoint accompanies it. B570 remains blocked, and the bottleneck-directed 358H intervention never reaches formal performance evaluation. A characterization paper need not optimize successfully, but its acceptance argument must rest on the consequence of the observations rather than an implied resolved systems bottleneck.

**D. Guard utility.** The paired secondary result is negative: Guard/Always-Spec latency is 1.0270760219826773, with 95% prompt-bootstrap interval [1.0129560055428655, 1.0435592653980876]. Guard is slower on this frozen comparison. Its 1.3114x target-relative speedup and 180/180 direct-ID agreement demonstrate feasible execution; fixed speculation's 1.3469x speedup removes a deployment rationale based on incremental aggregate latency improvement. Fallback activity, 24 Guard-faster prompts, and the hindsight oracle establish neither risk reduction nor transferable utility. The interval excludes session and recalibration variation.

**E. Scientific payoff and practitioner consequence.** The actionable consequence is specific: an exact-output deployment should qualify the actual committed-ID path; the tested fresh RTX serialized path passes, the tested B570 fallback does not, and isolated NPU throughput should not justify the measured 358H integration. These are useful counterexamples with better evidence than generic advice. Yet one model pair, short single-request workloads and co-varying stacks provide no transferable predictor of qualification or profitability, shared root cause, universal certificate, or performance remedy. Accounting, numerical path dependence, state reuse and adaptive speculation are already established topics in the paper's own framing. The new qualification evidence sharpens their manifestation more than it advances their explanation.

**Weak Reject, 2/5** on a scale of 1 Strong Reject, 2 Weak Reject, 3 Borderline, 4 Weak Accept, 5 Strong Accept. **Confidence: 4/5**, where 1 is low and 5 high; confidence is stronger in the bounded systems assessment than in predicting committee preferences.

The replicated qualification boundaries materially improve validity and empirical coherence, but do not materially overturn the principal v0.5 Weak Reject reason: limited scientific increment without a transferable causal/predictive result or successful performance remedy. The missing paired Guard inference is resolved in the unfavorable direction. Weak acceptance for careful empirical characterization remains defensible, but my independent judgment is that these narrow, credible findings do not yet clear the selective general contribution bar.
